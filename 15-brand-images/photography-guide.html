Brand Guidelines

Photography Style Guide

A comprehensive reference for creating and maintaining the SHUSMA visual identity across all photography. Every image must communicate our values of refined luxury, timeless elegance, and understated sophistication.

01

Photography Style Overview

The SHUSMA photography aesthetic is defined by a warm, golden, luxurious, and aspirational visual language that resonates with our discerning clientele. Every photograph should feel as though it was captured in a moment of effortless elegance, evoking the feeling of stepping into a world of refined taste. Our imagery draws inspiration from natural warmth and the gentle glow of golden-hour light, creating an inviting yet elevated atmosphere across all brand touchpoints.

Consistency is paramount — whether shooting on-location at a heritage estate or in a controlled studio environment, the final image must feel unmistakably SHUSMA. Compositions should prioritize clean negative space, purposeful prop placement, and a restrained color palette that lets the product remain the hero. Avoid trends or overly stylized effects that may date the imagery; our visual identity is built on timelessness.

The emotional tone of every photograph should communicate aspiration without exclusivity — our customers should see themselves in these images and feel inspired. Think warm hospitality meets high fashion, where every detail from fabric texture to ambient light has been carefully considered to reinforce the luxury positioning of the brand.

02

Lighting Guidelines

Golden hour is the preferred lighting condition for all on-location SHUSMA photography, as it naturally produces the warm, diffused quality that defines our brand aesthetic. When shooting in a studio, replicate this warmth using light sources in the 3200K to 4000K color temperature range, ensuring the scene feels inviting rather than clinical. Soft, diffused lighting is essential across all formats — employ large modifiers such as silk scrims, octaboxes, or window light to minimize harsh shadows and create a gentle gradient across the subject.

Avoid direct flash, hard-edged spotlights, or any lighting setup that creates deep, unflattering shadows on the product or model. The key is to maintain a luminous quality where highlights feel warm and shadows remain open and readable, never blocked up or muddy. Side lighting at approximately 30 to 45 degrees is recommended for product shots to reveal texture and dimension in fabrics, while front-diffused lighting works best for model portraits to ensure even, flattering skin tones.

03

Color Grading

Color grading is the final layer that unifies all SHUSMA imagery into a cohesive visual system. Shadows should carry a subtle warm undertone — push the hue of dark tones slightly toward amber or burnt sienna without making them appear muddy. Highlights should retain a golden luminance, sitting in the range of our Light Gold (#E2CC8A) to create a sense of radiant warmth in bright areas such as skin highlights, fabric sheen, and metallic accessories.

Midtones should lean toward a clean cream (#FAF8F5), serving as the neutral anchor of the image and ensuring that product colors read accurately against the overall warm cast. Apply the following HSL adjustments as a starting point: reduce blue saturation by 15 to 20 points, increase luminance in the orange and yellow channels by 5 to 10 points, and slightly lift the shadows while pulling back on pure blacks. The result should feel like looking through a pane of gently warmed glass — noticeable in its beauty, never heavy-handed.

\u2713 Recommended

  • Warm amber shadows with open detail
  • Gold-tinted highlights on skin and fabric
  • Clean cream midtones for accurate color
  • Subtle, cohesive grade across all images

\u2717 Avoid

  • Cool, blue-tinted shadows or overall cast
  • Blown-out or clipped highlights
  • Heavy contrast that crushes shadow detail
  • Oversaturated or unrealistic color palettes
04

Product Photography

All product-only shots must be photographed on a clean cream or white background to maintain a consistent and premium presentation across the ecommerce experience. The default camera angle is the three-quarter (3/4) front view, which provides the most informative and flattering perspective of each garment while showcasing its silhouette, drape, and construction. Supplementary angles — front, back, side, and detail close-ups — should follow the same lighting and grading standards to ensure visual harmony on product detail pages.

Gold accent props such as thin geometric stands, minimalist trays, or subtle fabric drapes may be used sparingly to add visual interest and reinforce the brand identity without distracting from the product itself. Maintain a consistent scale across all product thumbnails by standardizing the camera-to-subject distance and focal length for each product category, so that images sit uniformly on grid pages and category layouts.

05

Model Photography

SHUSMA celebrates beauty in all its forms, and our model casting should reflect a diverse range of backgrounds, skin tones, body types, and ages that align with our inclusive luxury ethos. Models should embody confidence and poise — poses must feel natural and self-assured rather than overly posed or editorially forced, conveying the idea that wearing SHUSMA is an expression of personal elegance. Shooting locations should feature luxury or aspirational settings such as heritage architecture, refined interiors, sunlit gardens, or high-end hospitality spaces that complement the clothing without competing for attention.

Jewelry and accessories on set should be kept to an absolute minimum — the SHUSMA garment is always the focal point. If accessories are necessary for styling, they must be understated and in gold or neutral tones consistent with the brand palette. Hair and makeup should follow a "less is more" philosophy: glowing skin, natural brows, soft matte or satin lip tones, and hair styled in an effortless manner that enhances rather than dominates the frame.

06

Post-Processing

All SHUSMA photography should be processed through a standardized Adobe Lightroom workflow to ensure consistency across every image produced by internal teams and external partners. Begin by applying the brand-approved base preset (see preset description below), then make targeted adjustments for exposure, white balance, and lens corrections on a per-image basis. The final grade must pass a visual spot-check against our reference images before delivery.

The SHUSMA Lightroom preset applies a warm tone curve that lifts shadows into the amber range, adds a subtle golden tint to highlights, and slightly desaturates blues and greens to keep the overall palette cohesive. Retouching standards are strict and non-negotiable: skin retouching is permitted to smooth temporary blemishes, even out uneven tones, and reduce under-eye circles, but body alteration of any kind — including reshaping, liquifying, or proportion adjustments — is strictly prohibited. Our brand stands for authentic beauty, and every image must honor the real form of the person wearing our creations.

\u2713 Retouching Allowed

  • Blemish and spot removal
  • Even skin tone correction
  • Under-eye circle reduction
  • Color and exposure consistency
  • Background cleanup and dust removal

\u2717 Retouching Prohibited

  • Body reshaping or liquifying
  • Proportion or limb adjustments
  • Skin smoothing that removes texture
  • Altering facial features or bone structure
  • Any modification that changes body appearance
07

Image Specifications

All final deliverables must meet a minimum resolution of 2000 pixels on the longest side to ensure sharp rendering across desktop, tablet, and mobile displays, as well as retina-quality zoom on product detail pages. Accepted file formats are JPEG (quality 85 or higher) for general use and WEBP for web-optimized delivery; raw files must be archived separately. Every image file must follow the standardized naming convention without exception: category-productid-angle.jpg (e.g., dresses-DR4021-front.jpg, accessories-AC1087-detail.jpg).

Color space should be sRGB for all web-deliverable files to ensure accurate display across browsers and devices. Maintain an organized folder structure mirroring the naming convention, and always include a metadata tag with the shoot date, photographer, and product SKU for future traceability. Images that do not meet these technical requirements will not be accepted into the brand asset library.
